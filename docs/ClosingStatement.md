
# Closing Statement

So in the end this became a game about repetition, but a specific _kind_ of repetition. A repetition informed by ritual and attentiveness. Obviously a certain attention is required in this game, as it is in most (all?) games, but the obfuscation of the interface, where the player has to rely more heavily on sound and subtle changes in the visuals, cause that attention to be intensified. 

_"It's shocking how rewarding the text reveal feels. People are immediately excited to see it, and it definitely seems to be enough of a payoff for them to keep going, which is a happy unexpected thing. I didn't plan on a "sticky" experience, but just this little bit of reward makes it work."_

I find it noteworthy that what started out as a simpler version of [Lest Ten Horizons Cry](http://www.mouseandthebillionaire.com/lthc) is more successful. Both projects deal with hiding content, but this project, with less artifice on top of the searching mechanic and arguably a smaller payoff, works better. It is as if all of the extra layers added on top of LTHC distracted form the core mechanic. The reward feels smaller, but by keeping that discovery-plus-outcome loop tighter, we foster the ritual of repetition. 

_"I am reminded of labyrinths, where the act of walking, of being in the moment, the process, the act, is the whole point."_

And it is also noteworthy that this repetitive ritual seems to be in contrast with how most games present themselves. Mario exists to reach the end of the level. Toad nudges us forward in our quest saying, "our princess is another castle," not "hey why don't you stay here and just jump around smashing things for a bit." This is not even to mention all of the other metagoals that get laid on _top_ of most games. High scores, collectibles, achievements, and the like incentivize the _completion_ of a task. The completion of a goal. But _Instauratio Exiguus_ never ends. There is no game over screen.

In _The Disappearance of Rituals_, Byung-Chul Han says, 

*"Rituals \[,t]hrough their self-sameness, their repetitiveness, they stabilize life. They make life last. The contemporary compulsion to produce robs things of their endurance: it intentionally erodes duration in order to increase production, to force more consumption. Lingering, however, presupposed things that endure."* (3)

It is this lingering that I believe many games lack. Being objects of consumption rather than attention, they become somehow less. _Instauratio Exiguus_ works because it demands an attentive stance. The subtly unintuitive nature of the whole experience means that you either give it your full attention or walk away. And this exact thing has perhaps been one of the hardest parts of this whole endeavour: by making games that _demand_ attention, you are guaranteed to alienate those who don't want to give that kind of attention. People expect their games to work for _them._ To entertain *them*. 

All of this makes me wonder how a _daily_, ritualistic playing of the average game plays in to this. By repeating our gameplay at certain times of the day do we get the same effect? My daily coffee and NYT crossword is certainly a morning _ritual_, but do I attend to it in the same way.[^1] Sudoku served this purpose during the pandemic where the need to apply some semblance of order to the world felt especially therapeutic, but did it "stabilize life?" Did it "make life last?" A little bit, yeah. Can a weekly Call of Duty game with friends fit in this mold?

Finally, moving forward with these ideas, I want to focus on two ideas/thoughts. First, can we get the same/similar experience out of a daily ritual game? A game where the repetition is happening further apart. Every day, week, month, etc. [^2] Secondly, because I want to show this at [Whaaat!?](whaaat.io) in November and it would be nice to leverage the old LTHC controller that is already there, can I translate this, easier, less fussy version into a four dial experience? And if so, what might those other dials control? It could be something as simple as overall color, graininess, etc. Or maybe there are additional movement parameters (zoom, rotation, etc)? Or maybe there are two nodes? 

Onward, as ever.
